Most people think of landscaping as a spring job. In Guelph, early fall is often better. The soil is still warm from summer, the nights are cool, and fall rain does a lot of the watering for you.

## What works well in the fall

### New sod
September and early October are great for laying sod. Cooler temperatures mean less stress on the grass and less watering than a July install. Aim to have sod down at least four to six weeks before the ground freezes so it can root.

### Overseeding
Late August to late September is the best window to seed a thin lawn. Warm soil helps seed sprout fast, and weeds are slowing down, so new grass has less competition.

### Mulch and beds
Fresh mulch in the fall insulates plant roots through freeze and thaw cycles, and your beds look finished first thing in spring.

### Trimming and removal
Once deciduous trees and shrubs drop their leaves, it is easier to see branch structure and remove dead or crossing limbs.

## What to leave for spring

- **Trimming spring-flowering shrubs.** Lilacs and forsythia set next year's buds in summer. Cutting them in fall removes the flowers.
- **Heavy fertilizing late in the season.** A fall feed is good, but applying fertilizer on frozen ground wastes it.

## Your fall checklist

1. Clear leaves off the lawn so they do not smother the grass over winter.
2. Keep mowing until the grass stops growing, and drop the height slightly on the last cut.
3. Overseed thin spots by the end of September.
4. Top up mulch in beds.
5. Cut back perennials that tend to get mushy, and leave the ones with seed heads for the birds.
6. Book your spring cleanup early.
