New sod looks finished the day it goes down, but underneath it is still a set of rolls sitting on soil. For the next four weeks, your job is to help the roots grow down into the triple mix. Water is what makes that happen.

## Week 1: keep it soaked

Water the new sod within an hour of installation, then keep it wet every day.

- Water deeply enough that the soil **under** the sod is moist. Lift a corner to check. It should feel like a wrung-out sponge.
- On hot or windy days, water twice: once in the morning and again in the early afternoon.
- Pay extra attention to edges and corners. They dry out first.

## Week 2: start rooting

Keep watering daily, but let the surface dry slightly between waterings. Gently tug on a corner. If it resists, roots are starting to grab.

## Week 3: water less often, but deeper

Cut back to every second day, with longer, deeper waterings. This pushes the roots down to find moisture and builds a stronger lawn.

## Week 4: move to a normal schedule

Water two or three times a week, adding up to about 2.5 cm (1 inch) of water, including rain. A tuna can on the lawn makes a simple rain gauge.

## Your first mow

Mow once the sod is firmly rooted and the grass is about 8 cm tall, usually around the two to three week mark.

- Set the mower high. Never take off more than a third of the blade.
- Make sure the blade is sharp so it cuts cleanly instead of tearing.
- Skip the mow if the ground is soggy, since the wheels can leave ruts.

## Common mistakes

1. **Watering lightly every day in week 3 and beyond.** Shallow watering makes shallow roots.
2. **Walking on it too soon.** Keep kids, pets and foot traffic light for the first two weeks.
3. **Letting the seams dry out.** Gaps between rolls shrink and brown if they dry, so soak them.
4. **Fertilizing right away.** The triple mix already has what the sod needs for the first month.
