Lumpy lawns are common in Guelph, where freeze and thaw cycles, settling soil and busy critters all leave their mark. Most uneven lawns can be levelled without tearing them out.

## Sand or soil?

- **Topsoil or a soil and sand mix** is the safer choice for most home lawns, especially with the clay-heavy soil found in parts of the region. It holds moisture and nutrients.
- **Pure sand** drains fast and is easy to spread, but a thick layer of sand over clay can create drainage problems. Use it sparingly, mixed with soil.

A 50/50 blend of screened topsoil and sand is a good middle ground for top dressing.

## For shallow dips (under 2 cm)

1. Mow the lawn short.
2. Spread a thin layer of your mix over the low areas.
3. Work it into the grass with the back of a rake or a push broom so the blades poke through.
4. Water it in.
5. Repeat every few weeks if needed. Never bury the grass under more than about 1 cm at a time.

## For deeper holes (2 cm or more)

1. Cut an H shape through the turf over the low spot with a flat spade.
2. Peel back the flaps of sod.
3. Fill underneath with soil and firm it down.
4. Lay the sod back down, press it in and water well.

## For high spots

Do the same thing in reverse: peel back the sod, remove soil to bring it level, and lay it back down.

## When to start over

If most of the lawn is uneven, full of weeds or mostly bare, levelling and patching can cost more effort than it is worth. Removing the old lawn, adding triple mix, grading for drainage and laying new sod gives you a flat, healthy lawn in one go.
